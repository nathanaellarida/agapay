Opening a Local Small Business in Cebu / Lapu-Lapu — Founder's Playbook
Source: Compiled from DTI Negosyo Center handouts, Cebu City BPLO Citizen's
Charter, and Lapu-Lapu City Citizen's Charter.

Who This Guide Is For
---------------------
First-time founders launching a physical business — a cafe, food stall,
barbershop, dental clinic, retail shop, salon, or co-working spot — anywhere
in Metro Cebu.

The 6-Phase Launch Plan
-----------------------

Phase 1: Validate the Concept (Week 1–2)
- Pick a clear customer niche. "Coffee shop near IT Park young professionals"
  beats "general coffee shop."
- Run 10–15 customer interviews. Skip "would you?" questions; ask about
  current spending behavior.
- Estimate first-year operating cost. The rule of thumb in Cebu: PHP 250,000
  to PHP 1,500,000 to launch a small physical shop, depending on size and
  fit-out.

Phase 2: Choose Your Legal Structure (Week 2)
- Sole Proprietorship — register with DTI. Fastest, cheapest. You own
  everything but you are personally liable.
- Partnership / Corporation — register with SEC. Best if you have co-founders
  or plan to raise outside capital.
- Cooperative — register with CDA. For community-driven groups.

Phase 3: Secure the Location (Week 2–4)
- Sign a Letter of Intent before a final lease — it locks the property while
  you finish due diligence.
- Verify Zoning Compliance with the City Planning and Development Office
  (CPDO). Some streets in Cebu are residential-only.
- Negotiate: 2 months deposit + 1 month advance is the local norm; some
  landlords accept lower for longer leases.

Phase 4: Government Registrations (Week 3–6)
The Cebu / Lapu-Lapu sequence is:
1. DTI Business Name Certificate — online, same-day.
2. Barangay Business Clearance — same-day if zoning is clean.
3. Mayor's Permit (a.k.a. Business Permit) — 3–7 working days through the
   Business One Stop Shop (BOSS) in the city's BPLO. You'll also clear:
   - Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC)
   - Sanitary Permit (City Health Office)
   - Locational / Zoning Clearance
4. BIR Registration — Form 1901 for sole proprietors, plus your books of
   accounts and an Authority to Print receipts.
5. SSS / PhilHealth / Pag-IBIG — register the business as an employer.

Phase 5: Build Out and Brand (Week 5–10)
- Fit-out cost in Cebu: PHP 5,000–15,000 per square meter for a coffee shop;
  more for restaurants with grease traps.
- Lock down social handles (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Google Business
  Profile) before opening — Google Business Profile is a free traffic engine.
- Photograph the space professionally before you open. Pre-launch hype is
  cheaper than post-launch ads.

Phase 6: Soft Launch and Iterate (Week 10–12)
- Run a 1-week soft launch with 30–50% off for friends, family, and the
  surrounding offices. The goal is feedback, not revenue.
- Track three numbers obsessively:
  * Daily customer count
  * Average ticket size
  * Repeat-customer rate after 4 weeks
- Make one operational change per week based on what you learn.

Common First-Year Mistakes
--------------------------
- Skipping the Mayor's Permit. LGU inspectors do walk-throughs; an unpermitted
  business will be padlocked and fined.
- Underestimating fit-out time. Construction in Cebu takes 1.5–2x longer
  than scheduled, especially during the rainy season.
- Hiring too early. Wait until the soft launch tells you the actual demand
  pattern before adding staff.
- No POS system. Even a free Loyverse or basic Foodee POS gives you the
  data needed to make smart inventory decisions.
- Ignoring SSS/PhilHealth registration. These come up the first time an
  employee gets sick, and the back-fees compound monthly.

Helpful Local Resources
-----------------------
- Negosyo Center Cebu City — free consultations, Mondays to Fridays.
- DTI Region 7 — runs frequent Kapatid Mentor ME workshops.
- Cebu City BPLO BOSS counters during January renewal season — fast-tracked
  one-stop processing.
- USC, UP Cebu, and CIT-U incubators — for tech-flavored small businesses.
