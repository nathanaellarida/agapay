DOST Startup Grant Fund (SGF) — Frequently Asked Questions
Source: DOST-PCIEERD Startup Grant Fund Program Guidelines.

What Is the Startup Grant Fund (SGF)
------------------------------------
The Startup Grant Fund is a non-equity, non-collateralized cash grant
provided by the Department of Science and Technology through PCIEERD
(Philippine Council for Industry, Energy, and Emerging Technology Research
and Development). It supports innovative, technology-driven Filipino
startups during the prototype, validation, and early scaling stages.

Who Can Apply
-------------
- Philippine-registered startups (DTI sole prop, SEC corporation, or
  cooperative).
- Operating for less than five (5) years.
- Have a working prototype OR a clearly defined R&D-driven product /
  process /business model.
- The Filipino founder team must hold majority ownership.

Funding Tiers
-------------
- Ideation Grant:    up to PHP 100,000  (proof of concept, problem
                                          validation, customer interviews)
- MVP Grant:         up to PHP 1,000,000 (build a minimum viable product
                                          and run a paid pilot)
- Scaleup Grant:     up to PHP 5,000,000 (post-product-market-fit growth,
                                          regional expansion, partnerships)
Funding amounts are indicative; actual approvals depend on the call.

What Costs Are Eligible
-----------------------
Grants typically cover:
- Software development costs (in-house or outsourced).
- Hardware prototyping and 3D printing.
- Laboratory testing and certification fees.
- Marketing pilots and customer acquisition experiments.
- Salaries of full-time R&D staff (capped percentage).
- Mentorship and accelerator participation fees.
- Travel for pitching at international demo days (limited cap).

What Is NOT Covered
-------------------
- Personal expenses, founder dividends, or back-pay.
- Office rent unrelated to R&D activities.
- Acquisition of land or vehicles.
- Repayment of existing loans or debt.

Application Process
-------------------
Step 1. Register your startup on startupphilippines.gov.ph and obtain
        accreditation under RA 11337 (Innovative Startup Act).
Step 2. Watch the DOST-PCIEERD website for an open call (typically two
        cycles per year, January and July).
Step 3. Submit the application package, which includes:
        - Project proposal (template provided by DOST).
        - Pitch deck (10–15 slides).
        - Financial projection for 3 years.
        - Founder bios + ID copies.
        - Proof of registration (SEC Articles, DTI Cert, or BIR 2303).
        - Letter from a Startup Enabler endorsing the application
          (optional but strengthens the case).
Step 4. Pre-evaluation by PCIEERD technical staff (4–6 weeks).
Step 5. Pitch to the Startup Grant Fund Committee — 10-minute pitch + 15
        minutes Q&A.
Step 6. Decision and contract drafting if approved.
Step 7. Tranche disbursement based on milestones agreed in the contract.

Reporting Requirements
----------------------
- Monthly progress reports during the grant period.
- Quarterly financial liquidation reports (with receipts).
- Final terminal report and impact assessment at the end of the grant.

Common Reasons for Rejection
----------------------------
- Lack of a clear "innovation" angle — DOST is not a generic MSME loan
  window. Submissions need a defensible technology or process novelty.
- Weak market validation — at least show waitlist signups, LOIs, or paid
  pilot customers.
- Missing or sloppy financial projections.
- Founder team without a technical co-founder for tech-heavy proposals.
- Applying as an unregistered "idea" rather than a real legal entity.

Pro Tips
--------
- Apply through a DOST-accredited Startup Enabler (e.g., QBO, IdeaSpace,
  AIM Dado Banatao Incubator). Their endorsement letter materially helps.
- Focus the pitch on impact metrics: jobs created, IP filed, customers
  served, export potential.
- Be realistic. The Committee prefers "credible 10x" over "fantastical 100x."
