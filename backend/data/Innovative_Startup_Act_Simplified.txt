The Innovative Startup Act (Simplified Guide for Filipino Founders)
Source: Republic Act No. 11337 — "Innovative Startup Act" (2019), DOST,
DTI, and DICT joint implementing rules.

What Is the Innovative Startup Act
----------------------------------
RA 11337 is the Philippine government's flagship law for supporting tech and
innovation-driven startups. It was passed in 2019 to give early-stage Filipino
founders access to grants, tax breaks, mentorship, and visa programs they
would otherwise have to chase down individually.

Who Counts as a "Startup" Under the Law
---------------------------------------
A "startup" under RA 11337 is any business or registered partnership that:
- Has been operating for less than five (5) years.
- Is engaged in the development of an innovative product, process, or
  business model that uses technology in a novel way.
- Is registered in the Philippines.
This is intentionally broad — software, hardware, biotech, deep tech,
fintech, agritech, and even creative-tech startups all qualify.

The Three Lead Agencies
-----------------------
Three government agencies share oversight, each focused on a different angle:
1. DOST (Department of Science and Technology) — funds R&D heavy startups.
2. DTI (Department of Trade and Industry) — handles business-side incentives,
   trade promotion, and the Philippine Business Hub.
3. DICT (Department of Information and Communications Technology) — supports
   software, AI, and digital startups, plus the Philippine Startup Ecosystem
   Development Program.

Key Benefits for Founders
-------------------------
1. Startup Grant Fund (SGF)
   - Cash grants distributed by DOST through PCIEERD, intended for prototype
     development, market validation, and scaling.
   - Typical range: PHP 100,000 to PHP 5,000,000, depending on the call.
   - You apply through the Startup Grant Fund Committee. Required documents
     include a project proposal, founder's pitch deck, financial projection,
     and proof of incorporation or DTI Business Name Registration.

2. Startup Venture Fund (SVF)
   - Co-investment vehicle: government matches private capital invested in
     qualifying startups, lowering the risk for early VCs.
   - Administered through the National Development Company (NDC).

3. Startup Visa Program
   - Allows foreign founders, employees, and investors to live and work in
     the Philippines under simplified immigration rules.
   - Issued by the Bureau of Immigration in coordination with the host
     startup business and a "Startup Enabler" (an accredited incubator,
     accelerator, or VC).

4. Subsidies and Fee Waivers
   - Discounts on filing fees with SEC, DTI, and IPOPHL (Intellectual
     Property Office).
   - Waived or discounted fees for registration with FDA and BFAD for
     food-tech, health-tech, and similar startups.

5. Mentorship + Access to Pinoy Startup Ecosystem
   - Free mentorship via QBO Innovation Hub, IdeaSpace, and university
     incubators (UP, ADMU, DLSU, USC, MSU-IIT).
   - Access to government-backed co-working spaces.

How to Get Started (3 Steps)
----------------------------
Step 1. Register your startup either as a sole proprietorship with DTI or
        as a corporation with SEC. Most VC-track startups choose SEC because
        it allows shares and equity rounds.
Step 2. Apply for accreditation as a "Startup" or "Startup Enabler" through
        startupphilippines.gov.ph. Accreditation unlocks access to all the
        incentives listed above.
Step 3. Submit grant applications when calls open (DOST publishes them
        quarterly). Pitch decks must include traction metrics, even if
        small (waitlist signups, MOUs, pilot customers).

Common Mistakes
---------------
- Applying for a grant without first being accredited under RA 11337.
- Treating the startup like a regular MSME — many grants and visa programs
  are exclusive to RA 11337-accredited entities.
- Skipping incorporation. SEC corporations can raise equity; DTI sole
  proprietorships cannot.
